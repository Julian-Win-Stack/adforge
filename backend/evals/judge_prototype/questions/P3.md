Question P3, person not pasted onto the room: do the presenter and the room look like one real photo taken by one camera, or like a person photo placed over a background photo?

Check each of these and say what you find for each:
1. Light: does the light on the person come from the same side, with the same colour and softness, as the light in the room (windows, lamps, shadows of furniture)? An evenly lit, front-lit studio look on the person in a room lit from a window or from one side is a mismatch.
2. Shadows and contact: does the person cast a shadow or block light on the floor, wall, counter or furniture near them, and touch or lean on anything in the room the way a real person standing there would? A person who stands in front of the room touching nothing and leaving no trace on it is a mismatch.
3. Camera angle: is the person seen from the same height and angle as the room? For example the room seen from above (looking down at the floor) while the person is seen straight on at eye level is a mismatch, and so is a floor or counter whose lines don't run behind the person the way they would.
4. Focus and detail: does the sharpness fall off with distance the way one lens would? A person crisp at every edge, with a room that is blurred or smoothed like a backdrop at a distance where it would still be sharp, is a mismatch.
5. Edges and scale: a hard outline, halo or glow around the hair or body, or a person too big or too small for the doors, counters or furniture next to them.

Fail when two or more of these are mismatched, or when any one of them is plainly wrong (a visible halo, wrong scale, a person floating above the floor, a clear mismatch of camera angle).
Pass when the person and the room look like one photo on these checks. A selfie or close shot where the room is only a soft blur behind the face passes check 4 if the blur is the strength a phone camera at that distance would give.
