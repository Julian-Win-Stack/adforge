Question C8, cream or liquid behaves like the real thing: does any cream, gel, foam, liquid or powder in this clip look and behave like the real product?

How to check:
1. Look: compare its colour and texture with the real product's photos (the product itself, or swatches or skin shown in them).
2. Follow cause and effect frame by frame. The amount of substance must follow what is done to it:
   - When it is squeezed, pumped or poured, you see it come out, and the amount where it lands (a palm, skin, a glass, a surface) grows by about what came out. A stream that keeps flowing while the amount where it lands stays the same fails.
   - It doesn't appear on a surface, grow, shrink, vanish or change colour or texture on its own, without being dispensed, applied, spread or wiped.
   - Where it lands reacts the way the real material would: a liquid or powder entering water spreads, swirls or dissolves; a cream rubbed or dabbed spreads, thins or blends where it is touched. A substance that doesn't respond to the hand, tool or surface touching it fails.
   - The thing releasing it moves the way that release needs (a tube pressed, a pump pushed down, a packet tipped). A flow while nothing causing it moves, in an otherwise frozen scene, fails.
3. It must pour, drip or spread like the right kind of material (a lotion doesn't run like water, a foam doesn't turn into soap suds).

Fail when any of these is plainly wrong on the main action. Pass when it looks and behaves like the real material.
Real product behaviour that looks odd passes (some serums are stringy, some foams are stiff).
"doesnt_apply" when no cream, gel, foam, liquid or powder appears.
