Question A12, presenter feels like a real person: do the presenter's face and voice in this talking clip feel like a real person talking to a friend, not an actor reading an ad?

Watch and listen. First describe, in plain words: (1) how the voice delivers the line: chatting like a person telling a friend, with changes in speed, pitch and stress that follow the meaning; or reading an ad script, with an even, measured, announcer-like delivery, every phrase landing the same way; (2) what the face does: shows a feeling that changes with the words (surprise, delight, relief), or holds one fixed smile or blank look; (3) what the body does: the head, shoulders and hands move as people do while talking, or they are held still with only the mouth moving; (4) whether the lips match the words and the eyes blink and look around naturally; (5) whether the presenter looks cut out and placed onto the room (edges, light on her not matching the room's light).
Fail when any of these feels fake: an ad-read delivery instead of chatting; a flat, robotic or monotone voice; a voice that doesn't fit the face; a fixed expression that doesn't change with the words; a body held still with only the mouth moving; lips that don't match the words or a mouth frozen while the voice goes on; a frozen, waxy face or one that moves in ways a face can't; eyes that don't blink or stare blankly; a presenter who looks pasted onto the room.
Pass when face, voice and body together feel like a real person chatting. "doesnt_apply" when no presenter talks in the clip.
