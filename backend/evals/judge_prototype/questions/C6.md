Question C6, nothing impossible: does anything in this clip move or change in a way that can't happen?

Look at the product, the hand using it, what it is used on, and the presenter. Video models often break physics in small, quick ways that a glance at a few frames misses, so check step by step before you answer:

1. Follow every moving part on the main action from the first frame to the last: where it starts, the path it takes, where it ends, and what moved it.
2. Sides: a part that ends up on the other side of a body part or a solid object that stayed in its way has passed through it, even when the moment it crossed is hidden behind a hand or happens between two frames. It may only get there by going around an edge (over, under or beside it). A part fixed at both ends (a strap, loop or handle) moves as one piece: follow its whole length, not only the end nearest you.
3. Strands and tools: hair, fibres, fabric or a cord that meets a solid tool must go around it or between its open parts (bristles, teeth, slots). Strands that run into the solid body of the tool and come out the other side passed through it.
4. Nothing moves by itself: anything that enters the frame, leaves it, opens, slides or lifts must be moved by a visible hand, arm or person, or move the way it would on its own (falling, pouring, rolling, a motor the product really has). Do not invent a holder you can't see. A part that jumps to a new place, or changes shape, between two neighbouring frames with nothing touching it moved by itself.
5. Flow: something that flows, pours or squeezes out while the hand and the container stay frozen, as in a photo, and the container never empties or changes as it would, is impossible.

Fail when any step finds a break, or when something appears from nowhere or vanishes, an extra copy of the product appears, a container refills itself, an item switches hands with no hand-over, a body part changes, hands or fingers merge, multiply or bend in ways they can't, or something rigid bends.

This question is about movement and things changing, not about how the product looks: printed text, labels or logos that blur or reshape do not fail it.

Do not explain a flaw away: if you see it in the frames, it counts, even when it lasts a fraction of a second. Pass only when every movement on the main action could happen in a real video. Flaws only in the background pass (Rule A).
