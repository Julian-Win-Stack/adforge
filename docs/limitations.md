# Limitations

What AdForge deliberately does not do yet. Don't build these without Julian's go.

- **B-roll doesn't show the result the voice claims.** The presenter can say "94% saw fewer wrinkles in 30 days" while the B-roll only shows the serum going on. Showing real results is next-level ad work, and we don't know yet whether it sells more. (Decided 2026-10-10.)
- **A run stopped by a failing start picture doesn't cancel work already running.** When a B-roll start picture fails its check 3 times, the run stops and the producer starts nothing new until the shop owner writes again, but other scenes' pictures, audio or clips already being made still finish and are paid for. (Picture judge build, 2026-10-10.)
- The start-picture judge checks the product and the objects it touches. It does not catch a wrong background object, such as a toilet that isn't a normal toilet (4 graded cases, 0 of 5 caught by every model tried, Oct 10).
- **No stop button for a fix.** Once the shop owner says yes to a plan, its steps run, finish and are paid for. A newer plan for a scene whose step is still being made starts once that step is done. (Route B step 1, Julian 2026-10-11 21:44; fix redesign 2026-10-17.)
- **Nothing says a fix worked.** The producer can't see pictures or videos, and code only shows the new ad, labelled with the change. A fix's new starting picture or clip is checked for the change (#131), but a pass is the judge's word, not proof. (Route B step 1, 2026-10-11; #131, 2026-10-13.)
- **The fixing agent trusts the clip judge.** When the judge fails a B-roll clip, the agent films tries until the judge passes one or it gives up. A wrong pass ends its tries on a bad clip, and a wrong fail can make it give up on a good one (prototype run, Oct 18: 5 of 10 cases went wrong this way, all the judge's mistakes). Every try stays a version the shop owner can pick. (Fixing agent build, Oct 19.)
- **Code stops the fixing agent in two places, as technical limits.** It gives up on a scene after 40 turns (a loop guard against an agent that never films or never stops), and it refuses a new line that takes over 15 s to say, as the video model makes B-roll clips of 5 to 15 s only. Neither is a judgement about the scene: the agent still decides when to give up. (Fixing agent build review, Oct 19.)
- **No escalation on a repeated note.** When a fix didn't work and the shop owner notes the same scene again, the same step is made again with the new fix, never a bigger one. Decided to test in rounds 4 and 5 first. (Julian 2026-10-11 21:53.)
- **A new line's clip is made again.** When the shop owner changes what a scene says, the scene keeps its starting picture and how its clip moves, but its voice, transcript and clip are made again and paid for. A new line too long for the scene is a can't on the plan. (Route B step 1, #138, 2026-10-12; a new line keeps the picture, Julian 2026-10-17.)
- **HeyGen ignores motion text on talking clips.** A talking scene's presenter can't be told to do an action (pick up the product, point, turn): HeyGen makes the talking clip from the line and picture and ignores any motion asked for. So an action in a talking scene is a "can't": a movement fix on a talking scene is refused, and only its starting picture or its line can change instead. (Tested 2026-10-11: one clip, Julian's grade "no"; runs/heygen-motion-test/.)
- **The shop owner's photos are for scenes that show the product only.** A photo sent with a note on a scene where the presenter talks is a can't on the plan. (Owner photos in notes, D7, 2026-10-15.)
- **A photo can't change the person.** "Our presenter should look like her" is a can't-yet: the person is made once and a photo never changes how they look, and a clean picture drawn from a photo never copies a face. (Julian 2026-10-15 17:45.)
- **No example videos.** The shop owner can send photos of their own things with a note, never a video to copy. (Julian 2026-10-15, Q6.)
- **A photo of something other than the product, sent before the ad is planned, isn't added.** Before the plan every photo is a product photo, so the producer asks them to send it with a note on a scene once the first version is made. (Julian 2026-10-15 17:55.)
- **About half the B-roll prompt-writer rules were never graded.** A rule is one sentence in the instructions the prompt writer or the planner gets. "Graded" means Julian watched a clip or picture made under it and said good or bad. The rules below came from a design talk or an audit, and no clip made under them has been graded. They may help, hurt or do nothing. Dropping them one at a time and grading enough ads to tell is about 20 rules times several ads at $1.40-3 each plus grading time, so it is not done now. They stay as they are; a rule that shows up behind a graded fail in round 5 or later gets tested then. Full list with marks: the "How a B-roll scene is made" page (Oct 17). Never graded: "shows" decides which movement is filmed; show the product, don't describe its looks; never write "no sound", "no speech", "no text"; follow the producer's note, owner chat for wishes, facts only from what is shown; take only the product from the chosen photo, the setting in words; an unusual pose is said once with its effect on the label; owner clean copies win over other pictures; putting the product on before a tool is never filmed; a screen result has no numbers or words; showcase shows no result the page doesn't prove; three kinds, no fit means not B-roll; a finishing step never gets its own scene; a B-roll line has at least about 10 words; at most 5 pictures per scene; the product-category guide; clip from a note keeps the picture and changes only the movement. (Decided 2026-10-18; the judge-remake writer's rule went with the remakes, Oct 19.)
- **I have sometimes hardcoded a rule in a prompt when one product failed.** That fixes the one product, does nothing for the next, and can break others. The right way: find the cause behind the fail, check it holds for more than one product, and fix that cause with a rule that would be true for a product never seen. (Julian 2026-10-18.)
- **Some shops can't be read at all.** In the Oct 19 test of 126 product pages, 12 gave no photos: TikTok Shop and Firecrawl refuse each other, and Chewy, Target, Temu, an Amazon page and a few small shops showed our reader a block or robot-check page instead of the product. Their ads can't be made from the link today; asking the owner for the page text and photos is designed separately. (Page photo + video picking, 2026-10-19.)
- **A before/after demo that lives only in a YouTube video on the product page is never seen.** The app reads gallery videos only (the ones placed with the product photos), and YouTube refuses video downloads from a server (tested 2026-10-19: forbidden twice, the proxy wasn't the blocker). Example: Lemi Shine Shower + Tile Cleaner, whose gallery video is a 2.7 s bottle clip and whose before/after demo is a YouTube embed in the page body, so its B-roll has no before/after view (failed rounds 1 and 2). (Julian 2026-10-19.)
- **Long gallery videos are skipped.** A gallery video over 50 MB (about 3 minutes at normal web quality) is not downloaded, so nothing in it reaches the planner. None of the 19 gallery videos in the Oct 19 test was over 60 s. (Julian 2026-10-19.)
- **A page the reader can't open needs the shop owner's paste.** When the page can't be fetched, or its text is a loading screen, cookie wall, bot check or error page, the shop owner pastes the page's text and attaches its photos in the page panel, or sends a working link. The app doesn't make such shops readable itself, and the shop owner can't send videos there. (Julian 2026-10-19.)
- **An ad can't start with no product photo.** The page panel needs at least one photo until the build that draws the whole product from the page text for the shop owner to approve. (Julian 2026-10-19.)
- **When the shop owner goes ahead without a photo of a part the product needs, the ad can lose all its B-roll.** The planner asks for a photo of something no photo shows, such as how the product looks out of its pack. If the owner goes ahead without one, the planner often plans every scene as the person talking, instead of filming the product used where that part isn't seen. A prompt line saying "keep only that part out of view" was tried once and didn't change it. (Planner general rules build, Julian 2026-10-19 19:34.)
- **A thin, hand-held product can be filmed being used without being told how it is held.** The planner films an everyday use anyone can picture without asking, but its plan names only the use, not which hand holds what and what moves. For a thin thing held in both hands, the video model can then draw it wrongly, such as one strand turning into two. No rule for writing how it is held was added. (Planner general rules build, Julian 2026-10-19 19:34.)
